# Phase 2.5 — Commercial UX & Visual Reality Check

**Audit date:** 2026-07-30  
**Baseline:** `docs/commercial-architecture-homepage-spec.md` and `docs/phase-2-commercial-architecture-implementation.md`  
**Runtime status:** Runtime visual validation unavailable in current environment.  
**Production-code changes:** None

## 1. Executive Verdict

Phase 2 is commercially stronger than the previous portfolio-led architecture. A qualified buyer can now identify that Venus Bridge Media serves Chinese companies, works in the UK, covers brand and launch activity, and can coordinate local execution.

The strongest improvement is the move from supplier categories to customer situations and business objectives. The strongest evidence is the automotive launch and location work. The truth controls remain unusually disciplined for this category.

The principal weakness is pacing. The homepage currently asks the visitor to process three consecutive commercial-information sections—12 text-led units in total—before the first real project image appears:

1. four Customer Situations;
2. four demand-recognition points;
3. four Solution rows.

The Hero uses an abstract production-system diagram rather than real work. On mobile, this makes the upper page feel like a strategy presentation before it feels like an execution company. Several visible sentences also explain internal governance—“not a strategic success case,” “not a proprietary methodology,” “not a partner logo wall”—instead of communicating customer value.

The site has not become a full market-entry consultancy, but it has acquired a partial consultancy tone. The correct next action is a narrow pacing and copy-compression pass, not another architecture redesign.

**Readiness verdict:** Ready for real B2B traffic. P1 conversion improvements are recommended; no P0 defect blocks traffic.

## 2. What Category the Website Now Feels Like

Closest category:

> **D. UK Brand & Launch Partner**

Secondary perception:

> A commercially aware UK production partner with some market-readiness language.

It no longer feels mainly like a photography/video studio. Real automotive launch images, bilingual positioning and the local-delivery model move it beyond a production-only identity.

It does not fully feel like a market-entry consultancy because:

- execution is repeatedly named;
- the strongest public proof is real launch and production work;
- professional-advice boundaries are explicit;
- no university, legal, accounting or partner ecosystem is invented;
- no strategic outcomes are claimed.

It occasionally looks like a consultancy landing page because:

- the Hero visual is a diagram of service routes;
- three dense information sections precede real work;
- maturity labels and proof-governance explanations are public;
- the page contains multiple frameworks and numbered grids.

## 6. Commercial Narrative Review

The narrative logic is correct:

> Situation → recognition → solution → proof → delivery → capability → conversion

The practical problem is that recognition and explanation occupy too much of the upper page before evidence appears.

The story is strongest from Proof onward:

- image-led execution cases;
- a clear five-stage process;
- local execution clusters;
- selected industry visuals;
- separate next steps.

The story is weakest where it exposes architecture or governance:

- public “Developing capability / Core delivery” labels;
- “not strategic success cases” language;
- “not a proprietary methodology” language;
- “not presented as a partner logo wall” language.

These statements are truthful but sound like notes from an audit or board deck. They should remain encoded in data and detail-page boundaries, not dominate commercial homepage copy.

## 7. Content Density Review

### Upper-page density

Before the first real project image, the visitor receives:

- two Hero paragraphs and two CTAs;
- eight paragraphs across four situation cards;
- four demand statements;
- four Solution descriptions, four maturity labels and four links.

This is manageable on desktop but excessive on a 390px screen.

### Repetition

Frequently repeated concepts include:

- UK / 英国;
- local delivery / 本地执行;
- objective / 目标;
- uncertainty / 不确定性;
- coordination / 协调;
- solution / 解决方案;
- evidence and claim boundaries.

The repetition protects positioning, but the visible result is less premium and less confident.

### Recommended density target

- Customer Situations: one short signal and one outcome per card, not two equal paragraphs.
- Demand: keep four points; do not expand to seven.
- Solutions: keep four, but remove public internal maturity terminology.
- Governance: one quiet boundary note at most on the homepage.
- Proof: allow imagery and titles to do more of the work.

## 8. Visual Rhythm Review

Current rhythm:

> Diagram → text cards → text grid → text rows → real images → timeline → text grid → quiet text → image cards → taxonomy cards → conversion text

The page has more structural diversity than a conventional SaaS template, but its first half still follows:

> TEXT → CARDS → TEXT GRID → TEXT ROWS

The first major visual release is the Proof section. After that point, rhythm improves substantially.

### Strong patterns

- editorial split in Demand and Network;
- image-led asymmetric Proof grid;
- five-column process sequence;
- four distinct industry images;
- quiet dual-conversion ending;
- restrained black, ivory and champagne system.

### Weak patterns

- abstract Hero route diagram resembles a strategy framework;
- many numbered systems appear in succession;
- Proof and Process are consecutive dark sections and may merge visually;
- Expertise imagery is followed immediately by another industry taxonomy grid;
- four industry cards use the same dimensions despite different evidence maturity.

## 9. Image / Proof Review

### Media distribution

Homepage real imagery:

- three automotive Proof images;
- four industry images: automotive, fashion/beauty, entertainment and technology;
- no real Hero image.

This distribution correctly prioritises commercial environments and keeps portraits from dominating. The problem is timing, not quantity: all real imagery arrives after three dense sections.

### Proof images

**London Automotive Brand Story Film**  
The aerial automotive gathering shows scale, location and an active environment. As the largest card, it is less immediately legible as a formal B2B launch than Changan or BYD and includes a prominent venue mural that could distract from the stated business objective.

**Changan European Brand Launch**  
The image clearly shows vehicles, brand environment, lighting, guests and a launch venue. It is the strongest immediate B2B launch proof.

**BYD BD11 London**  
The image clearly shows the bus, audience, stage, speaker and London context. It strongly supports local launch documentation.

### Proof card information

The cards correctly show:

- proof tier;
- project title;
- location and year where available;
- business objective;
- one confirmed Venus role.

They remain editorial rather than becoming mini case reports. This is appropriate.

### Technology

The robotics exhibition image is strong capability evidence: it shows a physical product display and clearly differentiates Technology from software development.

### Recommended proof adjustment

Do not move the full Proof section automatically. First test one of:

1. replace the abstract Hero canvas with an existing execution-led visual;
2. add a compact three-project proof cue immediately after Customer Situations;
3. use Changan or BYD as the visually dominant first Proof card.

## 10. Entertainment Balance Review

Entertainment remains visible through:

- the Entertainment & Culture industry card;
- dedicated capability pages;
- creator and live-production language;
- the broader talent and creator execution system.

It no longer dominates:

- the homepage Proof set is automotive-led;
- the Hero positioning is business-led;
- Entertainment is one of four image cards;
- capability media does not enter Work automatically.

The current homepage Entertainment image is an editorial portrait. It is visually distinctive but proves editorial production more than live execution. If later testing shows the site still feels too photography-led, a live event or stage image would communicate commercial scale more directly. This is a media-selection P2/P1 test, not a truth defect.

**Verdict:** Entertainment is appropriately visible and is no longer the company identity.

## 11. Customer Situation Review

### Exploring the UK

Clear for teams that need readiness and dependency clarification. It risks sounding advisory if separated from an explicit first practical test.

### Preparing a UK Launch

The clearest situation. Launch story, live plan and delivery are distinct and commercially relevant.

### Building UK Presence

Useful, but semantically broad. It can overlap with Exploring because both describe uncertainty and continuity rather than a specific transaction.

### Ready for Local Delivery

Very clear for agencies and mature briefs. It is the strongest secondary-persona route.

### Overall

The entries function as self-identification rather than four service names. Their differences are strongest in the titles and decision lines. On mobile, the paired signal/decision paragraphs make each card heavier than necessary.

Recommended refinement:

- keep all four;
- shorten each signal to one line;
- make the decision line the visual emphasis;
- vary CTA labels by intended action rather than repeating “Explore this path.”

## 12. Demand Creation Review

Four points are the right number. The section creates recognition rather than fear:

1. global messaging does not yet work as UK-facing content;
2. distant and local launch decisions are split;
3. production, approval and usage questions surface late;
4. an event may fail to leave reusable content or a next step.

The first three are concrete and credible. The fourth is slightly absolute:

> “A single event happens, but no useful content or next step remains.”

A less sales-led version would describe the risk without assuming failure, for example: a launch needs a plan for reusable content and follow-up.

Do not increase the list to seven.

## 13. Solutions Review

The four-solution reduction is stronger than a long services catalogue:

- Brand & Localisation;
- Launch & Activation;
- Creative Production;
- Local Delivery.

Launch, Production and Delivery are easily understood. Brand & Localisation is broader and less proven.

The implementation truthfully labels maturity, but **Developing capability** and **Core delivery** are internal portfolio-management terms. They make the page feel like a capability matrix and give all four solutions visually equal weight despite unequal maturity.

Recommended refinement:

- keep the four-part architecture;
- visually prioritise Launch, Creative Production and Local Delivery;
- present Brand & Localisation as an available scoped layer, not a fourth equal operational pillar;
- keep Creator/Talent and Innovation/Knowledge as supporting capabilities, not top-level equal cards;
- replace “View solution” with customer-action language where possible.

## 14. Proof Review

### Visibility

Proof is fifth in the approved architecture but arrives late in physical scroll distance. The visitor must pass 12 text-led content units after the Hero before seeing a real project.

Desktop impact: noticeable but tolerable.  
Mobile impact: high.

### Quality

The selected Proof is strong:

- real venues;
- real audiences;
- vehicles and product context;
- London/Munich locations;
- confirmed production roles;
- no invented outcomes.

### Governance copy

The homepage statement that current projects are execution cases rather than unverified strategic successes is internally correct but commercially over-explained. The tier label and truthful card content already provide the control.

### Verdict

- Proof quality: strong.
- Proof truthfulness: excellent.
- Proof timing: too late.
- Proof card density: appropriate.

## 15. Network / University Truth Review

Truth controls are sound:

- no partner logo wall;
- no institution logo wall;
- no Cambridge or Oxford claim;
- no guaranteed expert access;
- no vetted legal or certified accounting network claim;
- specialist input is project-specific and adviser-appointed;
- institution records remain empty and gated.

The visual treatment is correctly understated and text-led.

The problem is tone. The heading:

> “Configured around the brief, not presented as a partner logo wall.”

and its Chinese equivalent refer to what the site refuses to fake. This is audit language, not premium customer language.

Recommended public expression:

- lead with “Built around the brief”;
- describe production and talent coordination positively;
- keep the independent-adviser sentence as a smaller boundary note;
- do not add logos or stock network imagery.

## 16. Industries Review

Evidence hierarchy:

- **Core:** Automotive & Mobility
- **Growth:** AI & Technology, Fashion & Beauty, Entertainment & Creator
- **Adjacent:** Selected Consumer Brands

The written hierarchy is correct. The visual hierarchy is not:

- all four Expertise cards use the same 4:5 size;
- all four receive equal column width;
- the separate Industry Priorities section then explains a hierarchy the image layout did not show.

Automotive should feel strongest before the visitor reads the tier labels.

Recommended refinement:

- consolidate the visual Expertise cards and the Industry Priorities taxonomy;
- make Automotive the dominant image or larger card;
- retain the three growth areas as equal secondary cards;
- keep Selected Consumer Brands as a quiet text link;
- do not imply mature sectors beyond the approved list.

## 17. Navigation Review

Visible navigation:

- How We Help
- Industries
- Solutions
- Proof
- Why Venus
- Contact
- persistent Fit Call CTA

### Strengths

- old capability sprawl is gone;
- Proof is explicit;
- Industries is canonical;
- Fit Call is persistent;
- no empty Insights item appears;
- mobile navigation is accessible and scrollable.

### Ambiguity

**How We Help** and **Solutions** are close concepts:

- How We Help is situation-led;
- Solutions is capability-led.

This distinction is sound in information architecture but not self-evident in a short navigation label. The current order also places Industries between them, weakening the conceptual relationship.

Do not merge them without behavioural evidence. First test:

- clearer mega-menu descriptions;
- ordering How We Help → Solutions → Proof → Industries;
- a Solutions label such as “What We Deliver” only if user testing confirms confusion.

### Mobile

The default expanded How We Help accordion gives the four situations good visibility. It also makes the mobile menu long before the user reaches Solutions and Proof. This is acceptable but should be confirmed in a real browser.

## 18. CTA Review

Primary commercial action:

- Request a 20-minute UK Fit Call.

Secondary commercial action:

- Send an Execution Brief.

### Density

The homepage has one dominant body-level gold button in the Hero, plus the persistent header version of the same action. Section links use lower-weight editorial links. The final conversion uses text links rather than two competing gold buttons.

CTA hierarchy is controlled and not aggressive.

### Clarity

Fit Call is clearly for teams still deciding the objective or main uncertainty. Execution Brief is clearly for projects with defined objectives, location, date and deliverables.

Potential improvement for the agency persona:

- expose “Already have a brief? Send it” as a quiet Hero or mega-menu text link;
- do not elevate it to a second primary gold CTA.

## 19. Contact Review

### Semantic distinction

The two tabs clearly explain:

- Fit Call: fit, objective and uncertainty;
- Execution Brief: defined project inputs and deliverables.

The interface truthfully says a Fit Call is a request, not instant booking.

### Visual distinction

Both modes use the same form shell, field style, spacing, submit-button treatment and two-column pattern. The Execution form is longer, but the two modes still look like variants of one form rather than two commitment levels.

### Fit Call density

The Fit Call collects:

- name;
- company;
- role;
- optional website;
- UK stage;
- objective;
- timing;
- main uncertainty;
- email;
- optional other contact;
- consent.

This is not a lightweight request. It is shorter than the Execution Brief but still demands substantial input before a 20-minute call.

### Execution Brief density

The Execution Brief appropriately asks for:

- objective;
- location and date;
- deliverables;
- channels;
- talent/venue/event needs;
- usage;
- approval owner;
- reference link;
- contact details.

### Recommended refinement

- keep the two distinct modes and server contract;
- make Fit Call visibly lighter through progressive disclosure;
- consider making role, website and other contact secondary/optional rows;
- retain stage, objective, timing and uncertainty as the essential qualification fields;
- keep Execution Brief fully structured;
- show a short “what happens next” line without promising an unapproved SLA.

### Strong Chinese

### Chinese that feels translated or audit-led

- “全球品牌信息尚未转化为可信的英国市场表达。”

### Recommended Chinese direction

## 22. Mobile Review

Static review at a conceptual 390px width:

### Strengths

- all major grids collapse to one column below `sm` or `lg`;
- Hero CTAs become full width;
- headings use clamped sizes and controlled character widths;
- mobile navigation uses a scrollable viewport;
- form inputs use full width and minimum touch heights;

- Proof and Expertise images keep controlled aspect ratios;
- final conversion stacks naturally.

### Risks

1. The Hero includes a minimum 29rem diagram after the text, producing a very tall first section.
2. Four Situation cards stack with two paragraphs and a CTA each.
3. Four Demand items stack as individual blocks.
4. Four Solution rows stack before any real project image.
5. The first real proof may appear more than 3,000px into the page.
6. The Fit Call still contains approximately ten visible inputs/decisions.
7. The default-open mobile How We Help accordion lengthens navigation.
8. Repeated section spacing and headings may create information fatigue even without overflow.

### Mobile recommendation

- introduce real proof earlier;
- compress Situation copy;
- keep Demand at four;
- reduce visible Solution metadata;
- make Fit Call progressively disclosed;
- verify sticky header, focus trap, long Chinese labels and textarea heights in a real browser.

## 26. P1 Improvements

### P1.1 Bring real execution proof forward

The highest-impact issue is the distance from Hero to real work.

Test one focused change:

- use an existing commercial execution image in the Hero; or
- add a compact proof cue after Customer Situations.

Do not duplicate the full Proof section.

### P1.2 Remove internal governance language from the homepage

Compress or relocate:

- “not strategic success cases”;
- “not a proprietary methodology”;
- “not a partner logo wall”;
- visible internal maturity labels.

Keep the actual data gates and detail-page boundaries unchanged.

### P1.3 Make Fit Call genuinely lower commitment

Use progressive disclosure or visually demote optional fields. Preserve the qualification logic but reduce the initial impression of a long application form.

### P1.4 Resolve industry duplication and evidence hierarchy

Combine the four image cards with Core/Growth/Adjacent hierarchy. Make Automotive visually dominant and keep Selected Consumer Brands quiet.

### P1.5 Clarify How We Help versus Solutions

Test navigation descriptions and order before considering any merge. Preserve URLs and the situation/solution distinction.

### P1.6 Reconcile Hero and customer architecture

The Hero canvas shows the old three project routes while the next section presents four customer situations. A future visual adjustment should not ask the first viewport to teach two different taxonomies.
